# Scientific Review: claims, evidence, and whimsy

Use this skill when you write or review emojibench captions and stories.

## The three categories

Label every scientific statement as one of these:

1. Observation: something directly shown in a source image or dataset.
   "The cell in the crop is elongated." The evidence is the image.
2. Interpretation: a reasonable reading of an observation, stated as
   such. "The elongated shape suggests the cell moved through tissue."
3. Creative analogy: whimsy for storytelling. "The macrophage is a
   superhero that saves the day." Never presented as evidence.

A whimsical illustration is never scientific evidence. Keep the three
categories distinguishable in every caption and story.

## Rules

1. Every observation claims a source: an asset id, a dataset, or a
   citation. Unsourced observations are rejected in review.
2. Use hedged language for interpretation ("suggests", "appears to"),
   and say what would change the reading.
3. Creative analogy is allowed and encouraged, but the text must not
   assert it as fact about biology.
4. Match the audience. "Kids" text: short sentences, no jargon,
   analogies to everyday things. "Researcher" text: precise terms,
   citations, measurements when available.
5. Do not claim a generated image depicts a real cell unless it is
   derived from a real microscopy source, with the source recorded.
6. Never let an automated check or vision model approve scientific
   claims. A human reviews before publication.

## Review checklist

For each panel or caption, ask:

- Is every factual claim an observation with a source?
- Are interpretations labeled as interpretations?
- Is the analogy clearly playful?
- Is the language right for the chosen audience?
- Would a biologist wince? If yes, fix or label it as whimsy.
